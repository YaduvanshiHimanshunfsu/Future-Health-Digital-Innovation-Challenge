title: "X-linked hypophosphataemia"
---
X-linked hypophosphataemia affects how the body keeps phosphate, which matters for bones and teeth. The gene sits on the X chromosome and follows a dominant pattern, so a single changed copy is enough and nobody is a carrier. How it passes on depends on which parent has the change. A mother has a pair of X chromosomes and passes either of them, so each child has a 1 in 2 chance. A father passes his only X to every daughter and to none of his sons. A specialist can explain what it means for somebody and what care is available.
